# Design DNA

This is the short, durable reference for the Website's visual and experiential direction. It records agreed principles, not every implementation detail. The direction is open for discussion in the current conversation.

## Conversation rule

When a new design principle or change is inferred from the conversation rather than stated as a clear decision, ask Angel whether it should be added here. Keep brainstorms and undecided ideas out of the agreed DNA unless Angel asks to preserve them as proposals.

## Current documented baseline

- **Purpose:** The public front door for Soft Strange Studio, introducing the studio and guiding visitors toward public notes, selected work, and studio context.
- **Character:** Minimal, warm, authored, gentle, and studio-like. The site should feel personal and welcoming rather than like a technical dashboard or generic product shell.
- **Materials and palette:** A sage torn-paper header, warm cream notebook-paper surfaces, a light dot-grid field, tactile paper edges, and soft visible shadows.
- **Identity and type:** A hand-drawn studio wordmark and brief handwritten accents add personality; clear, familiar reading typography carries the main content.
- **Composition:** Keep the homepage calm and direct. Secondary pages use a shared editorial language with a clear reading path and image-led paper pieces.
- **Imagery:** Use artwork and imagery that feel connected to the studio and the page content. Keep the visual system cohesive across Home, About, Notes, Portfolio, and Store.
- **Motion and interaction:** Movement should be gentle and purposeful, support orientation or atmosphere, and respect reduced-motion preferences.
- **Voice:** Use short, human-facing copy. Be honest about what is published, available, or still planned.

## Guardrails

- Keep the experience warm and distinctive without making it busy or hard to read.
- Preserve clear navigation and comfortable reading at desktop and mobile sizes.
- Keep internal publishing or implementation details out of visitor-facing copy.
- Do not imply that planned work, products, or inventory are already available.
